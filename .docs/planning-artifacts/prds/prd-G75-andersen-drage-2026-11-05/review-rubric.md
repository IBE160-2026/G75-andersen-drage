# PRD Quality Review — AI-Based Learning Assistant for Students (prd-G75-andersen-drage-2026-11-05)

Reviewed: `prd.md`, `addendum.md`, `.memlog.md` (D1–D9), and the brief's carried review findings, against `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`. Stakes: solo graded IBE160 project, deadline 15 Dec 2026, Fast path. The PRD sits at the top of the chain (UX, architecture, stories).

## Overall verdict
This is a strong PRD for its stakes. It has a clear thesis (an 80/20 core, grounded in the student's own text, run in a mastery loop), a precise Glossary, testable consequences on almost every FR, honest non-goals, and Delivery Requirements tied directly to the grading guide. Nearly all of the 82 carried review findings have landed as FRs or decisions. The real risk sits in the core loop's mechanics, which downstream stories will depend on most. Round size and the choice of Items within a Round are undefined (FR-18). "Try again" in FR-10 collides with the cache rule in FR-4. Passage segmentation and Key Concept ID assignment are unspecified, yet the Quote Check (FR-9) and progress carry-over (FR-4) both depend on them. All of these can be fixed in a paragraph each. None of them puts the thesis at risk.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Decisions are stated as decisions and tagged at the point of use (D1–D9, e.g. "Decision D3", "Decision D7" in FR-3, FR-9, §8.3). §8.3 Out of Scope names what each decision gave up: "A fuzzy or 'weak' Quote match tier (Decision D3)", "Splitting long Notes into chunks (Decision D7). They are rejected instead." The three Open Questions in §10 are genuinely open, and each names where it will be resolved (architecture, before delivery, before prompt tuning). There are no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts. With all assumptions confirmed, that is acceptable at these stakes.

The one dodged tension is what happens if the strict D3 choice does not meet its own target. SM-3 requires at least 80% of Items to pass an exact-substring Quote Check, and SM-C2 forbids loosening Normalization to get there. The PRD does not say what gives if that target is missed on the reference notes: the prompt, the model choice (Open Question 1), or the threshold.

### Findings
- **[medium]** No fallback when strict matching misses SM-3 (§9 SM-3, SM-C2; §8.3 D3) — The PRD commits to exact match and an 80% pass rate, and forbids looser matching, but it does not say what is traded if the reference notes fall short. That is the most likely point of tension during implementation. *Fix:* Add one sentence to §9 or §10. For example: if SM-3 is not met, first tune the prompt, then reconsider the model (OQ-1); lowering the threshold is a PRD update with a recorded reason (DR-4).
- **[low]** Latency bound depends on an undecided model (§5 NFR-4 "with the chosen model"; §10 OQ-1) — This is reasonable, but NFR-4 should be listed as an input to the model comparison so it does not get checked only after the choice is made. *Fix:* In OQ-1, list NFR-4 latency next to quality and schema adherence as selection criteria.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is no furniture. The Vision is specific to this product: "Every study item shows the Passage of the student's own note it comes from, and the app checks in code that the quoted text really is there" could not be pasted into another learning-app PRD. Sara is the only persona, and she drives UJ-1 and UJ-2, including the edge cases (scanned PDF, tab closed mid-quiz) that become FR-3 and FR-15. The JTBD "Trust" line maps directly onto FR-9 and FR-12. Most NFRs have product-specific bounds (NFR-4: 60 s / 15 s; NFR-5: at most 2 calls; NFR-7: 360 px). There is no claim of innovation.

### Findings
- **[medium]** NFR-6 is hedged and has no verification method (§5 NFR-6 "meets basic WCAG 2.1 AA expectations") — "Basic … expectations" is an adjective, not a bound. Design/UX is 10% of the grade, so the examiner needs to see how this was checked. *Fix:* Either say "meets WCAG 2.1 AA for the listed criteria", or keep the list and add a check: for example, zero critical axe/Lighthouse accessibility violations on every screen, plus a documented keyboard-only walkthrough of UJ-1.

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is clear and the features follow from it. Input rejection (§4.1) protects cost and trust. Grounding (§4.3–4.4) is the trust bet. Mastery and Rounds (§4.8) close the "spend my remaining time on what I got wrong" job. Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than activity: SM-1 coverage, SM-2/SM-3 grounding, and SM-4 runnability for the grading context. Counter-metrics are present, and they are pointed: SM-C1 ("More Flashcards … is not better") protects the 80/20 bet, and SM-C2 protects D3. The MVP is a problem-solving MVP, and the scope logic matches.

### Findings
- **[low]** "The most central 20%" in the Vision has no operational counterpart (§1 "turns the most central 20%"; §4.3 FR-6, FR-7) — The 80/20 idea becomes "5–15 Key Concepts" and "summary at most 25% of word count". Neither is 20%, and Centrality Rank has no defined basis. This is fine as rhetoric, but a reader may ask how "20%" is checked. *Fix:* Either soften the Vision ("the central core") or add one line saying that the 80/20 profile is realized through the FR-6 and FR-7 limits and is validated by SM-1.
- **[low]** SM-6 is thin evidence for the problem statement (§9 SM-6) — It is one self-test by the author, with no target. It is acceptable as a secondary metric, but it is the only metric that tests "time saved". *Fix:* Give it a pass condition, e.g. time to the first self-test is shorter with the app on at least 2 of 3 reference notes.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs are strong here. They have explicit messages, numeric limits, a full state table (FR-17) with "a unit test for every row", and edge cases (zero-eligible Readiness Score, fewer than 10 concepts in Panic Mode). The gaps cluster in the core loop. Those are the parts story creation and the DR-2 unit tests will lean on hardest, which is why the grade here is adequate rather than strong.

### Findings
- **[high]** Round size and Item selection are undefined (§4.8 FR-18; §3 "Round") — FR-18 gives an order (shaky, new, known) and says "Known concepts make up about 20% of the Round". It never says how big a Round is. Are all new concepts included, or a batch? Does "20%" mean 20% of the final Round or of the non-known part? It also never says which Items a Round uses: all 1–2 Flashcards per concept, and one or all Quiz Questions per concept (FR-7 gives "at least one")? FR-18 is listed in DR-2 as unit-tested, and a pure function cannot be written from this text. UJ-2 ("shaky concepts come first, followed by one concept she already knew") is the only example. *Fix:* Add consequences such as: "A Round includes every shaky and every new eligible concept; known concepts are added until they are ceil(20%) of the Round's concepts (min 1 if any exist); each included concept contributes all its surviving Flashcards and one Quiz Question, rotated across Rounds." Also give a worked example.
- **[high]** "Try again" after a thin Study Set conflicts with the cache (§4.3 FR-10 "a 'Try again' button that starts a new generation"; §4.1 FR-4 "When a reconnection hits the cache, no AI call is made") — The PRD does not say whether a below-floor Study Set, or a failed generation, is written to the cache. If it is, Try again and later re-uploads hit the same thin set. If it is not, re-upload costs another generation. FR-10's "Try again" also bypasses NFR-5's limit of 2 calls without saying so. *Fix:* In FR-4/FR-10, state that a Study Set below the FR-10 floor, or a failed generation, is not cached (or is cached, but Try again explicitly bypasses the cache). Also say that each Try again is a new generation under NFR-5.
- **[medium]** Passage segmentation is unspecified (§3 "Passage: a numbered segment of the Extracted Text"; §4.3 FR-9) — FR-9 fails "A Quote that spans two Passages". Whether Passages are paragraphs, sentences, or fixed-size blocks therefore directly drives SM-3's pass rate and FR-12's highlight. It also determines whether Passage IDs are stable for the same Note Hash. There is no deferral note to architecture. *Fix:* Either define it ("a paragraph of the normalized Extracted Text, split on blank lines; IDs are deterministic for a given Note Hash") or mark it explicitly as an architecture decision in §10 or the addendum, with the constraint that it be deterministic.
- **[medium]** Key Concept ID assignment is unspecified, so progress carry-over is not testable (§4.1 FR-4 "existing Mastery States are kept for Key Concepts whose IDs still match"; §4.3 FR-6 "a stable ID") — If the model emits IDs, two generations will rarely match, and the rule reduces to "progress is lost on regeneration". If IDs are derived (e.g. a slug of the normalized concept name), matching becomes meaningful. The FR-6 consequence ("do not change … for the same cache key") is trivially true because of the cache. *Fix:* State how IDs are formed (e.g. "a slug of the concept term, assigned in code"), or state plainly that regeneration resets progress, and simplify FR-4 to match.
- **[medium]** The prompt-injection test passes on any outcome (§4.3 FR-8 "still produces a valid Study Set (or a clean error)") — As written, the test cannot fail on the thing it is guarding. *Fix:* Add "and no Item contains the injected output X".
- **[low]** Untestable phrasings remain in a few consequences — FR-6 "scales with Note length" (no rule from word count to concept count); FR-7 "focused on the central content"; FR-16 "avoids jargon"; FR-8 "a clear error". *Fix:* For FR-6, give a rule (e.g. 5 + 1 per 1,000 words, capped at 15) or drop "scales" and keep only the range. Move "focused" and "avoids jargon" into the SM-2 spot-check rubric, where they can be judged by hand.
- **[low]** FR-22 has no consequences (§4.9 FR-22) — It is a Should, but if it is built, its import needs at least the FR-21 behaviour. *Fix:* Add "Import of a file with an unknown schema version or a different Note Hash is rejected with a message; import never crashes the app."
- **[low]** How Demo Mode is turned on is unspecified (§4.10 FR-23/FR-24; §6 DR-1 "Demo Mode is the documented default path"; FR-5 "the app offers Demo Mode") — It might be an env flag, a UI toggle, or automatic when no key is set. FR-24 ("Demo Mode is off") and FR-5 imply different mechanisms. *Fix:* One sentence in FR-23: how Demo Mode is selected, and that it is the default when no key is configured.

## Scope honesty — strong
§7 Non-Goals does real work, especially "The Quote Check proves that the source exists, not that the answer is right", which heads off the most likely over-claim. §8.3 lists exclusions with the decision behind each. The assumptions were handled cleanly: tags were removed after confirmation, and every value appears in §11 with its location and the date it was confirmed. Open-items density is low (3 Open Questions, 0 open assumptions, 0 PM notes), which is appropriate for a PRD meant to green-light building.

### Findings
- **[low]** Two silent omissions a student may assume exist (§4.9; §7) — There is no list of previously studied Notes (UJ-2 requires re-uploading the file to reconnect), and there is no in-app way to delete a Note's stored data (NFR-8 mentions "clearing storage" only as something to avoid). Both are reasonable for v1, but they are inferred rather than stated. *Fix:* Add both to §8.3 as `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` items, or add a one-line Should.

## Downstream usability — strong
The Glossary is the PRD's strongest asset. Nouns are capitalized and used consistently across FRs, UJs and SMs. Every feature section says "Realizes UJ-n". IDs are contiguous (FR-1–24, NFR-1–9, DR-1–6, SM-1–6, SM-C1–C2), and cross-references resolve (FR-13 → FR-17, FR-18 → FR-10, DR-3 → SM-1–3). Sections stand alone because they cross-reference through Glossary terms rather than "see above". `addendum.md` keeps architecture candidates out of requirements and maps them back to FR/NFR IDs. The drift that does exist is listed under Mechanical notes.

### Findings
- **[low]** "Not covered" acts as a fourth concept state outside the Glossary (§4.3 FR-10 "marked *not covered*"; §3 "Mastery State … *new*, *shaky* or *known*") — "Eligible" (FR-19) and "incomplete" Round (FR-15) are the same kind of term: used as states in testable consequences but not defined in the Glossary. Story and architecture work will need them as data-model fields. *Fix:* Add Glossary entries for "Not Covered" (or "Eligible Key Concept") and "Incomplete Round", and say that Not Covered is separate from Mastery State.

## Shape fit — strong
The shape matches: a consumer-style single-user app with real UX, so three UJs with a named protagonist are load-bearing and not excessive. §6 Delivery Requirements is a smart adaptation for a graded deliverable: each DR names its grading criterion and weight. Rigor is proportionate. There is no persona inflation and no market section.

### Findings
- **[low]** The DR weights read as if they add up across DRs (§6 DR-4 "Process, 30%" and DR-5 "Process, 30%"; DR-2 and DR-3 both "QA, 15%") — A reader may think Process counts for 60%. *Fix:* Write "(part of Process, 30%)", or say in the §6 intro that DRs sharing a criterion share its weight.

## Mechanical notes
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** clean. All 15 §11 entries point to locations that contain the value (e.g. "§4.3 FR-10" → FR-10 sits in §4.3). There are no stray `[ASSUMPTION]` tags.
- **Wording drift in §11 vs §9 SM-2:** §11 says "at least 90% of the spot-checked Items are correct". §9 SM-2 says the answer "follows from the cited Passage". "Correct" contradicts §7 ("does not claim that its answers are correct"). Align §11 with SM-2's wording.
- **SM-5 "passes in CI"** (§9) has no matching requirement. DR-2 asks for a documented command, not CI. Either add CI to DR-2 or change SM-5 to "passes with the DR-2 command".
- **UJ-1 → UJ-2 numbers:** UJ-1 ends with 8 Key Concepts and two of them *shaky*. UJ-2 shows a Readiness Score of 63% (5 of 8), which implies one concept is still *new* or not covered. Yet UJ-2's Round lists only shaky and known concepts. Either adjust the score (e.g. 75%) or mention the remaining new concept.
- **Consent timing:** UJ-1 says "Before her first upload she confirms". FR-5 says "Before the first Note is sent for generation". D8 in the memlog says "before first upload". These are compatible, since local extraction can happen before Consent, but pick one phrasing.
- **"Least recently shown"** (FR-18) needs a per-concept last-shown timestamp. That timestamp is not listed among the persisted data in FR-21.
- **Undefined minor terms:** "sample note", "text view", "prompt version" and "Centrality Rank" (how it is set) are used as nouns in testable consequences but have no Glossary entry. "Summary" (FR-7, UJ-1) and "Summary Point" are used interchangeably.
- **UJ-3 protagonist** is a role ("The examiner") rather than a named person. That is acceptable for a role-based journey and needs no change.
- **Required sections:** all present for the stakes (Vision, users and JTBD, UJs, Glossary, FRs, NFRs, Non-Goals, MVP scope, SMs with counter-metrics, Open Questions, Confirmed Assumptions).
